A Balanced Enterprise Architecture Framework for AI Adoption

Bhagyeshkumar Chokhawala

44th International RAIS Conference on Social Sciences and Humanities

Artificial intelligence is increasingly central to enterprise transformation, yet many organizations still approach adoption through disconnected pilots, vendor-driven experimentation, or narrow technology programs. This paper presents a balanced enterprise architecture framework for AI adoption that integrates business, technology, developer experience, and user experience perspectives with explicit security and ethical AI considerations. Governance and operating model provide the control and coordination needed to scale AI safely, responsibly, and sustainably. The paper introduces a TOGAF-style capability map and a practical execution toolkit covering readiness assessment, use case prioritization, governance gates, reference implementation assets, stakeholder conflict resolution, phased roadmap delivery, threat modeling, data protection, ethical impact assessment, bias review, accountability, and incident response. The proposed framework helps enterprises translate AI ambition into structured, governable, secure, and value-oriented implementation. It argues that enterprise AI maturity should be measured not only by the number of models deployed, but also by the alignment among business capability, technical readiness, engineering enablement, security assurance, responsible governance, and trustworthy human outcomes.

Active Inference as an Intrinsically Explainable Recommender Architecture via Epistemic-Pragmatic Score Decomposition

Bhagyeshkumar Chokhawala and Atif Farid Mohammad

44th International RAIS Conference on Social Sciences and Humanities

Explainability and user control remain major challenges in modern recommender systems, especially in neural and sequential ranking models where recommendation scores are often produced from opaque latent representations. Existing post hoc explanation methods may describe model outputs, but they often fail to reveal the actual decision criteria used to rank items. This limitation becomes more important as recommender systems must balance relevance, novelty, diversity, uncertainty, and popularity risk. This study proposes an intrinsically explainable recommender architecture grounded in Active Inference. The framework treats recommendation as a transparent decision process in which each item is evaluated through interpretable components related to preference alignment, information gain, uncertainty, and exposure risk. By making these components part of the scoring process itself, the approach provides explanations by design rather than after the ranking has already been produced. The proposed architecture also supports user control by allowing recommendation behavior to adapt to changing preference beliefs and exploration needs. Evaluation against standard recommender baselines shows that the approach can maintain competitive ranking performance while improving transparency and control beyond accuracy objectives. These findings position Active Inference as a promising foundation for explainable, adaptive, and auditable recommender systems.

Outcome-Centric API Design for AI-Enabled, Observable, and NoSQL-Consistent Service Architectures

Bhagyeshkumar Chokhawala

The 24th Int'l Conf on Software Engineering Research and Practice

Application Programming Interfaces (APIs) have become durable enterprise architecture assets because they connect channels, workflows, systems of record, data products, events, AI tools, and autonomous agents. Yet many APIs are still designed as screen endpoints, database wrappers, or isolated service calls. This paper presents a concise outcome-centric API design framework for service-oriented enterprise ecosystems. The framework aligns APIs with business outcomes and domain capabilities; applies lean API principles to reduce unnecessary surface area and payload waste; enforces role-aware authorization; supports AI-enabled access through Model Context Protocol (MCP) tools and agent-to-agent (A2A) collaboration; and preserves observability, selfhealth, testing automation, specification compliance, and end-to-end integrity. A distinctive contribution is the treatment of transactional and data integrity across multiple NoSQL entities and API layers. The paper explains how aggregate ownership, single-writer rules, optimistic concurrency, idempotency, outbox/ inbox messaging, process-state management, projection freshness, and reconciliation can be combined to support recoverable business transactions without assuming one global relational transaction. The framework is illustrated through a customer-disconnect scenario but is intended for broader enterprise workflows.

Active Inference as Multi-Attribute Decision Making: Explainable Planning via Expected Free Energy Decomposition

Bhagyeshkumar Chokhawala and Atif Farid Mohammad

28th International Conference on Artificial Intelligence

Explainability remains a central challenge in sequential decision-making systems, particularly those based on reinforcement learning and deep neural architectures, where learned policies often lack transparent internal structure. Post-hoc interpretability techniques approximate explanations after training but rarely expose the causal quantities that govern action selection. Active Inference offers an alternative foundation by framing perception, learning, and action as the minimization of Expected Free Energy (EFE), unifying epistemic uncertainty reduction and pragmatic goal alignment within a Bayesian framework [1,3]. This paper demonstrates that Active Inference can be rigorously reframed as an adaptive Multi-Attribute Decision Making (MADM) engine. We show that Expected Free Energy naturally decomposes into attribute-specific components—uncertainty reduction, risk sensitivity, preference alignment, and ambiguity minimization— making EFE minimization mathematically equivalent to weighted multi-criteria optimization. Furthermore, state-dependent belief updates induce dynamic attribute weighting, enabling context-sensitive trade-offs without explicit reward shaping. We formalize this equivalence, analyze interpretability properties, and introduce intrinsic explanatory artifacts including attribute-level EFE traces, bias heatmaps, and decision trajectory graphs. Comparative analysis with reinforcement learning and post-hoc explainability techniques demonstrates that Active Inference provides explanations by construction rather than by approximation. These findings position Active Inference as a principled framework for inherently explainable sequential decisionmaking systems.
